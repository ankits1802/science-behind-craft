# Page checklist: every craft, family and category page, and every answer page

General rules, learnt from the critics of the first consolidated page (2026-11-19; design 6.5,
chemistry 7). Each rule is written for any craft. The examples show how one craft applied it and
are never the rule itself. A page meets every rule before anyone scores it.

## First, derive the page's own lists from its dossier

Before building, write down three lists for this craft (or category). The rules below refer to
them.

- **Mechanisms**: the distinct pieces of science that make the craft what it is, one per
  transformation the reader would ask "why does that happen?" about. Find them from the
  dossier's stages, reactions and materials, never from memory. Examples by family: a dyed or
  printed textile has one per colour or per mordant-dye pair; pottery and terracotta have the
  clay body, firing atmosphere and any slip or glaze; metal has the alloy, casting or forming,
  and the patina or inlay; stone has the rock and how it is worked; wood and lacquer have the
  timber, seasoning and finish; cane, bamboo and grass have the fibre and its treatment; painting
  has the ground, the pigments and the binder.
- **Ambiguous terms**: every word in the dossier or its kin crafts that names two different things
  (a common name for two substances, a craft word that is also a chemistry word, a local name
  shared by two plants). Examples: lime the fruit and lime the mineral; soda as sodium carbonate
  and soda-lime glass; lac the insect resin and lacquer; "black" pottery from reduction firing
  and black from a pigment.
- **Signature fact**: the one moment that defines the craft for a reader. Examples: cloth turning
  from green to blue in air; a bell-metal casting rung for its tone; clay turning red or black in
  the kiln.

## The first minute

1. **A short headline, never a raw question.** An answer page edits the reader's question into a
   display line, with the full wording behind "Show the full question". The raw question appears
   nowhere else either: not in Cite this, metadata or titles. A craft page opens on its name, its
   native name and a one-line lede.
2. **The best photograph is in the first viewport**: the masthead picture beside the lede on
   desktop and under the name on phone, cropped from its focal box so people and work stay in
   frame. A stand-in picture says what it actually shows.
3. **One masthead.** No second card repeats the masthead's GI date, place or name.

## Counting and labels

4. **One count, and it is true.** Name sections and chapters consistently and count each once.
5. **Provenance counts only this page's own sentences.** Sources borrowed from the family, the
   category or kin crafts are counted and numbered separately.

## Repetition

6. **The signature fact is the hero once.** It is explained where it happens (the opening figure,
   the mechanism's player or explainer); everywhere else it is a link to that figure. Alternative
   tellings (comic, film subtitles, the student version) are the only exception.
7. **Each picture appears once, and no two pictures look alike** (same scene, person,
   photographer and object). Replace one, or crop it to a clearly different subject, credited as
   cropped.
8. **The media chapter is a gallery**: illustrations, video and film keyed to the section titles,
   with no text retold.
9. **One citation panel** with tabs (this answer, the monograph), not stacked blocks.

## Complete at rest

10. **No progress state at rest**: no "Writing section n of m", percentages or "n of m scenes".
    The page rests finished, and any replay is opt-in.
11. **Feedback is visible where the reader is**: toasts are fixed to the viewport.
12. **Sticky bars are opaque and never overlap** each other or the chapter rail.

## Layout

13. **No dead zones.** A picture shares its row with a quote, figure or caption; a framed box is
    sized to its content; a chapter lead never floats over an empty field.
14. **A credit sits under its own figure.**
15. **A card with no sentence is not drawn.** A kin-craft or shared-concept panel joins crafts
    only through sentences that use the term in the same sense (see rule 18).
16. **The story closes before the tools**: a short coda (a practitioner's quote over the finished
    work), then the reading tools compact, then sources. Keep the page under about 47,000 px on
    desktop; past that, the last third reads as an appendix.
17. **Charts are drawn to scale**, from zero where the quantity is a depth, length or count.

## Science and chemistry

18. **Keep ambiguous terms apart.** Every term on the ambiguous list is used in one sense per
    sentence. Sentences and kin panels are never joined across senses.
19. **Every transformation says what changes and what does it**, at the level the page teaches:
    bonds, oxidation states, phase, crystal structure, moisture or temperature. It names which
    species is oxidised or reduced, what acts as mordant, flux, binder or catalyst, and what heat
    or air does. When a source's loose wording would hand a role to the wrong species, the
    sentence follows the chemistry and the quote stays as it is.
20. **Every mechanism on the list gets equal depth**: a drawing or structure plus its key step and
    a cited sentence for each. The thinnest mechanism sets the page's level, so none is left at
    one line.
21. **Equations are balanced for atoms and charge, and quantities are plausible** (units,
    magnitudes, temperatures) before anything is drawn.
22. **A source error stays verbatim and is marked**: [sic], with the correct formula or value
    beside it. The same quote renders the same way everywhere on the page.

## Every field, densely (owner, 2026-11-20)

23. **Every field a source covers is on the page**, in the chapter where a reader looks for it:
    overview and history in the opening chapters, process and materials with the making, chemistry,
    physics and botany with the science, geography, geology, climate and ecology with the place,
    economics, society, design and policy with the people and the market, health beside the stage
    or material it concerns. An answer page draws on every field its question touches, through one
    expert seat per field, and its provenance names the fields it consulted.
24. **Each field is dense**: three or more cited sentences that each add a quantity, a mechanism, a
    name or a date, plus a figure, table, fact grid or quantity where a source gives numbers. No
    field is one sentence long.
25. **Every screen earns its height.** Margin notes, fact grids, small multiples and tables carry
    detail beside the prose instead of below it; no empty band, no decorative spacer beyond the
    spacing scale, and no card that repeats a sentence already on screen.
26. **A missing field is research, not a hole.** It never appears as an empty heading or a "not yet"
    note; it appears as a question that starts cited research, and as open work on the MCP server.

## Process

- An owner-approved page is served as built at its route (decision 0100), not rebuilt.
- After building, one critic reads the page in a browser, design and science together, at 360 and
  1440 in light and dark, and scores it against this checklist. Fix everything that keeps it below
  9, then re-score once. No judge panels.
